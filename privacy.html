Foilcase — Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 November 2026

Foilcase is a collection tracker for trading cards. It has no accounts and no sign-up, and the only analytics it has are about the subscription itself. This page describes exactly what the app does with data, including the parts that are easy to leave out.

What stays on your device

Your collection — which cards you own, what you paid, condition, grading, notes and any purchase dates — is stored in a database on your device. It is not uploaded anywhere. Deleting the app deletes it.

Photographs taken while scanning are processed on the device by Apple's Vision framework and are never saved or transmitted. Nothing leaves the camera.

The app keeps a local diagnostics log of measurements from scanning attempts — distances, timings and whether a guess was corrected. It contains no images and never leaves the device. You can clear it in the app.

Network requests the app makes

Apart from the subscription records described in the next section, the app makes three kinds of request, none of which carries anything about you — no identifier, no account, no description of your collection:

  • Card images, fetched from the public content delivery network that hosts the catalog's artwork.
  • Price files, fetched from a fixed address on GitHub Pages. The same file is served to everyone; the request does not say which cards you own.
  • The App Store, through Apple's StoreKit, to show the subscription and check whether yours is active.

As with any request over the internet, the servers involved — operated by their own companies, not by us — can see the network address it came from. That is a property of the internet rather than something the app sends.

Subscription records

The app reports its subscription to RevenueCat, a service that keeps subscription records for app developers, so that we can see how many people start a trial, subscribe, renew, cancel or ask for a refund. RevenueCat receives:

  • Your purchase history in this app: which plan, the price and currency, when it started, renewed or ended, and Apple's identifiers for those transactions, together with Apple's signed record of when and how this copy of the app was obtained.
  • Two identifiers that name this installation, not you: a random one RevenueCat creates the first time the app opens, and the one iOS gives every app from the same developer on your device (Apple calls it the identifier for vendor). Neither is your name, your email address or your Apple Account, and we hold no account, name or email address to connect them to. Deleting the app discards the first; the second is reset once none of our apps is left on the device.
  • Basic details of the request: the app's bundle identifier and version, the system version, the device model, your device's language and your App Store country, whether the app may ask to track you (it never asks, so iOS reports that it has not), and technical details of RevenueCat's own code, such as its version.

Nothing about your collection, your scans or your photos is sent. RevenueCat also checks that a purchase is genuine, and can confirm a subscription the device has not caught up with yet; it can never take away a subscription Apple says you have. We use these records only to run the subscription and to understand how it is doing. Where the law of the EU or the UK applies, we rely on our legitimate interest in doing exactly that.

RevenueCat keeps the records on our behalf and uses them only for us, under the data processing terms that are part of its agreement with every developer, and it protects them at least as well as this policy does. Its own privacy policy describes how.

How long subscription records are kept, and deleting yours

The records are kept for as long as Foilcase is offered, so that renewals, refunds and restores can be matched to the purchase they belong to. Deleting the app stops anything further being sent, but does not remove what was sent before.

To have your records deleted, email privacy@treamz.me with the order ID from the receipt Apple emailed you for the purchase (it starts with letters, such as MT2B3C4D5E). That ID is the only way to find your records, since we hold nothing else that names you. We delete them in RevenueCat, which removes the purchase history it kept for that installation, and reply when it is done. Deleting the records does not cancel the subscription: that is done in the App Store.

Payments

Payments are handled entirely by Apple. The app never sees, receives or stores a card number, billing address or any other payment detail, and neither does RevenueCat. Manage or cancel a subscription in the App Store; cancelling takes effect at the end of the period already paid for.

What the app does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics beyond the subscription records above, and no crash reporting. RevenueCat's is the only third-party SDK in the app that sends data anywhere.
  • No tracking across apps or websites.
  • No selling of data. The subscription records go to RevenueCat so that it can keep them for us, and nowhere else.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. It asks no one for personal information: no name, no email address, no account.
